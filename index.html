    12-Step Process

      Step 1The Meet & Greet

      Script

      "Welcome to SVG Motors. My name is _______ and your name? Nice to meet you (customer's name). Would you mind if I write that down? I have a terrible memory and don't want to forget your name. [Verify spelling of name]"

      "Here is my card so that you know who I am."

      "What would you like to accomplish today?"

      Possible Customer Responses

      Customer
      "Just looking."
      Response
      "Excellent! We encourage you to look around because we have a wonderful selection."
      Customer
      "Do you have a..."
      Response
      "You came to the right place. We have a wonderful selection, with 7 locations, I'm certain we have something that you'll like. It will take me a couple of minutes to search our inventories across all SVG locations."
      Customer
      "What's your best price?"
      Response
      "I'd be happy to find that out for you. It will take me a couple of minutes to do so."
      Customer
      "Can I get financing?"
      Response
      "You came to the right place. Would you like to take advantage of our Pre-Approval process?"

      Push the Curiosity Button

      Script

      "Before I do that, let me show you something you won't see anywhere else."

      The SVG Story

      Script

      "Here at SVG Motors we've been the satisfaction leader since 2013. We know from experience that we can't expect you to buy a vehicle from us unless we:

      • Treat you the way you want to be treated
      • Find a vehicle that you will love to drive
      • Make it easy to get the information you need
      • And fit the vehicle comfortably into your budget

      "We also know that in today's competitive market, we need to do more. That's why when you buy your vehicle from us, we give you the Superior Value Guarantee. Here (hand guest the SVG Handout) let me show you."

      "How does that compare to what you've seen so far?"

      Common Customer Objections & Responses

      Customer
      "I haven't seen anything like that."
      Response
      "I'm glad to hear that. Our Superior Value Guarantee really sets us apart. Can I make a suggestion that will make this easier and save you a lot of time?"
      Customer
      "I'm not sure yet. I'm still comparing different dealerships."
      Response
      "I understand completely. Taking your time to make comparisons is smart when making such an important decision. Based on what you've seen at other places, what features of our guarantee stand out most to you? [Listen to response] Thank you for sharing that. Can I make a suggestion that will make this easier and save you a lot of time?"
      Customer
      "The guarantee looks interesting, but I'm more concerned about finding the right vehicle first."
      Response
      "You're absolutely right to focus on finding the perfect vehicle for your needs. That's actually our priority too. Can I make a suggestion that will make this easier and save you a lot of time?"
      Every response transitions to: "Can I make a suggestion that will make this easier and save you a lot of time?"
      Note: Invite customers in for needs assessment & outline purpose, process & payoff. Begin a conversation to establish rapport and lead the guest to the next step in your process.

      Step 2Needs Assessment

      Determine what the customer is looking for in a vehicle.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Bridge from Step 1

      Script

      "My suggestion is this: let's sit down for a few minutes so I can learn what you're looking for. That way I'm pulling vehicles that actually fit, not wasting your time on the wrong cars. Sound good?"

      Discovery Questions

      Script

      Ask in order. Write the answers down on the needs-assessment sheet so the customer sees you taking it seriously.

      • "What are you driving today, and what do you like about it?"
      • "What would you change if you could?"
      • "Who's going to be driving this? Just you, or family too?"
      • "What's the primary use? Commute, road trips, kids, towing, work?"
      • "Any must-haves on the new one? Heated seats, third row, AWD, sunroof, specific tech?"
      • "Any deal-breakers? Things you definitely don't want?"
      • "What kind of monthly investment are you comfortable with?"
      • "How soon are you looking to be in the new vehicle?"

      Confirm What You Heard

      Script

      "So if I'm hearing you right, you need a [type] that does [primary use], with [must-haves], in the [budget] range, ideally [timeline]. Did I get that right?"

      Note: Don't pitch yet. The whole point of Step 2 is to listen. The pitch starts in Step 3 once you know what to pitch.

      Step 3Selection & Presentation

      Show appropriate vehicles based on customer needs.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Inventory Search

      Script

      "Give me a couple of minutes. I'm going to check what we have across all 7 SVG locations that fits what you described. I'd rather show you the right two or three than every car on the lot."

      Narrow to a Short List

      Aim for 2 to 3 options. One that hits the must-haves, one slight stretch with extra features, one safer or value pick.

      Present the Options

      Script

      "Based on what you told me, I pulled three. This [Year Make Model] hits everything on your list. This one is similar but adds [feature] that I think you'll appreciate given [reason from Step 2]. And this one is the value option if you want to stay closer to your budget."

      "Which one would you like to look at first?"

      Walk Out Together

      Script

      "Great choice. Let's walk over to it together. I'll grab the keys and we'll take a look."

      Note: Let them pick. Don't steer them to the highest-margin unit. The Superior Value Guarantee handles the value conversation later. Build trust now.

      Step 4Product Walkaround

      Highlight key features and benefits during a walkaround.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Six-Position Walkaround

      Walk the customer around the vehicle in a defined order. At each stop, tie one or two features back to something they told you in Step 2.

      1. Front. Grille, lighting, badging, safety tech (adaptive cruise, auto-brake).
      2. Driver Side. Wheels, profile, paint, driver door, trim level.
      3. Rear. Tailgate or trunk, cargo space, towing capacity if relevant.
      4. Passenger Side. Sliding doors, second-row access, family use cases.
      5. Under the Hood. Engine, MPG, powertrain warranty.
      6. Interior. Seat them in the driver's seat. Walk through tech, comfort, storage, second row.

      Feature, Advantage, Benefit

      Script

      "This [feature] means [advantage], which for you, since you mentioned [thing they said in Step 2], means [benefit]."

      Example: "This has Apple CarPlay (feature), so your phone connects wirelessly the moment you sit down (advantage), which means since you said you're on calls during your commute, you'll never have to fumble with cables again (benefit)."

      Land the Test Drive

      Script

      "Let's get you behind the wheel. The best way to know if it fits is to drive it. I'll grab a dealer plate."

      Note: Don't recite a feature list. Tie every callout to something the customer told you mattered. If you didn't get a Step 2 answer that justifies a feature, skip it.

      Step 5Test Drive

      Allow the customer to experience the vehicle firsthand.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Pre-Drive Setup

      • Copy of license, insurance card.
      • Dealer plate on the vehicle.
      • You drive first to get them out of the lot safely. Demonstrate one or two features in motion.
      • Pull over, switch seats, hand them the key.

      Hand-Off Script

      Script

      "Take the route you'd normally drive. Hop on the highway, take a turn or two, brake hard once so you can feel it. Treat it like it's already yours."

      While They Drive

      Stay quiet for the first minute. Let them feel it. Then, naturally:

      • "How does it feel compared to what you're driving now?"
      • "Notice the [seat bolster, steering weight, visibility]?"
      • "Try the [Bluetooth, lane assist, blind-spot]. Pretty seamless, right?"

      End-of-Drive Trial Close

      Script

      "On a scale of 1 to 10, how does this one fit what you were looking for?"

      If 8 or higher, move to Step 6 and start trial closes. If 7 or lower, dig in: "What's keeping it from a 9 or 10?"

      Note: Take the same route every time. Pick one with a smooth highway merge, a tight turn, and a stretch where they can feel the brakes. You'll know the vehicle's strong moments and time them.

      Step 6Trial Close

      Gauge customer interest and address any initial concerns.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Trial Close Questions

      Script

      Ask one. Wait for the answer. Don't stack questions.

      • "Can you picture yourself driving this for the next few years?"
      • "How does this compare to anything else you've looked at?"
      • "What color would you want it in?"
      • "Where would this live, in the garage or out front?"
      • "If we can find the right numbers, is this the vehicle for you?"

      If They Say Yes

      Script

      "Great. Let me grab the trade keys and your driver's license. I want to take a quick look at your current vehicle while my manager pulls together some numbers for you."

      If They Hesitate

      Customer
      "I'm just not sure yet."
      Response
      "Totally fair. Help me understand what's making you unsure. Is it the vehicle, the timing, or the numbers we haven't talked about yet?"
      Customer
      "I want to think about it."
      Response
      "Of course. Most people do. While you're thinking, what's the one thing you'd want to think through? If I can answer that right now, would it move you closer to a decision today?"
      Note: The trial close isn't a hard close. You're checking temperature. If they're not warm yet, go back to Step 4 or 5 and find what's missing.

      Step 7Trade-in Evaluation

      Appraise any trade-in vehicle if applicable.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Get the Vehicle to Appraisal

      Script

      "While my manager works on the numbers for the new one, I'd like to take a quick look at your trade. Do you have the keys and registration? It'll only take 10 to 15 minutes."

      Walk-Around with the Customer

      Do this with them, not without them. Note things openly. They already know about the dings.

      • Exterior: dings, scratches, paint, wheels, tires.
      • Interior: wear, smells, electronics, seat condition.
      • Mechanical: start it, listen, look at fluids, mileage.
      • Service records, second key, owner's manual.

      Manage Expectations Before the Number

      Script

      "A trade-in number is wholesale, not retail. Whatever you see on a private-sale listing is what someone would pay after spending their time, taking the risk, and dealing with strangers. Our number reflects what we can actually move it for at auction or recondition for our own lot. Fair?"

      Present the Number

      Script

      "Based on the condition, the mileage, and what the wholesale market is doing on this model, our offer on your trade is $X,XXX. That comes off the price of the new vehicle and reduces what you finance."

      Common Pushback

      Customer
      "KBB says it's worth more than that."
      Response
      "I get that, and KBB is a good starting point. The number you saw is probably private-party value, which assumes you sell it yourself. Our offer is wholesale, what an actual dealer or auction would pay today. The convenience of trading in here, plus the tax savings on the difference, usually closes most of the gap. Want me to show you the math?"
      Note: Don't argue the number. State it, explain the basis, then move to the math. The trade is one number on a bigger picture, not the whole deal.

      Step 8Initial Presentation of Figures

      Present the vehicle price and discuss available incentives.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Set the Frame

      Script

      "Mr./Mrs. Customer, I have your numbers ready. There's a lot on this sheet, so I'm going to walk through it once start to finish, and then take any questions at the end. May I begin?"

      Walk the Numbers

      Present in this order. Gesture to each line; do not read every number aloud.

      • MSRP / Retail price
      • Manufacturer rebates and incentives
      • Dealer discount
      • Trade allowance and trade payoff (net trade)
      • Government fees, doc fees, taxes
      • Out-the-door price
      • Three monthly investment options at different terms or down payments

      Bridge to Their Reaction

      Script

      "Looking at these three options, which one feels closest to what you had in mind?"

      If They Pick One

      Move to Step 9 only if there's a gap to close. If the option works as-is, go straight to Step 10.

      If They Push Back

      Customer
      "That payment is too high."
      Response
      "I hear you. Help me understand. Is the issue the monthly number, the down payment, the term, or something else? Once I know which lever to pull, I can see what we can do."
      Note: Present everything in the same calm cadence. Pausing or apologizing for a number tells the customer the number is wrong. Confidence in delivery is half the negotiation.

      Step 9Negotiation

      Work with the customer to reach a mutually acceptable deal.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Listen First, Always

      Script

      "Tell me where you need to be for this to work today."

      Then shut up. Write down the number. Don't react.

      Restate Their Position

      Script

      "So if I'm hearing you right, if we can get to $X per month at $Y down on a Z-month term, you'd take delivery today. Did I get that right?"

      Take It to the Manager

      Script

      "Let me take this back to my manager and see what I can do. Give me a few minutes."

      Bring back a counter that closes part of the gap, never the whole gap on the first pass.

      The Bracket

      If the customer's number is below where you can finish, bracket it. Counter higher than where you'll land so there's room to "give" on the next round.

      Common Pushback

      Customer
      "Another dealer offered me a better price."
      Response
      "I appreciate you sharing that, and I take it seriously. Do you have it in writing? I want to make sure we're comparing the same vehicle, the same trim, and the same fees, because the out-the-door is what actually matters. If the comparison is apples-to-apples, I'll do everything I can to earn your business today."
      Customer
      "I need to think about it overnight."
      Response
      "I understand. Before you go, can I ask: is it the vehicle, the numbers, or something I haven't covered yet? If it's the numbers, let me take one more shot at making this work tonight, since the rebates and rates we're using today aren't guaranteed tomorrow."

      When You're Stuck

      Script

      "Mr./Mrs. Customer, I want to be respectful of your time. We're $X apart. Can we split the difference and finish this today?"

      Note: The Superior Value Guarantee, the 7 days to love it, and the protection menu in Step 11 carry value the other dealer can't match on price. Always pull the conversation back to total ownership cost, not just the sticker.

      CARFAX Review

      (Mostly reading from CARFAX)

      Script

      "Now, let's take a look at your CARFAX Vehicle History Report which verifies the vehicle's history."

      Green Checkmarks  ·  Positive Indicators across:

      • Total Loss
      • Structural Damage
      • Airbag Deployments
      • Odometer Check
      • Accidents / Damage
      • Manufacturer Recall
      • Basic Warranty

      Reading the CARFAX Guarantee

      GUARANTEED — None of these title problems were reported by a U.S. state Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV). If you find that any of these title problems were reported by a DMV and not included in this report, you may qualify.

      Transition to the SVG Guarantee

      Script

      "But CARFAX isn't the only one giving guarantees. In fact, the G in SVG means we guarantee your satisfaction with our 7 days to love it promise. This is exclusive to SVG because we want to provide you with complete peace of mind. Our guarantee reflects the confidence we have in our vehicles and our commitment to your satisfaction. While we stand firmly behind the quality of our vehicles, we also understand the importance of planning ahead for your peace of mind."

      Setting Up Vehicle Service Contract Discussion

      Script

      "Based on your driving XXX miles per year, certain components might need attention after the manufacturer's warranty expires."

      Building Value Bridge

      Script

      "What I've learned is that the number one thing that creates a negative ownership experience isn't the vehicle itself, but unexpected repairs that weren't budgeted for. Those $2,000 to $3,000 surprises no one plans for."

      Solution Introduction

      Script

      "So, what I'd like to do, if you're open to it, is show you some options I've put together that my long-term customers have found really valuable in enhancing their ownership experience. Would that be okay?"

      Menu Presentation Setup

      Script

      "I've organized these options from our most comprehensive protection package, which many of my customers choose, down to what I believe is the essential minimum coverage you should consider. The great thing is, you can choose the level of protection that makes the most sense for you."

      Initial Objection Handling

      Customer
      "I don't want any of these add-ons."
      Response
      "I understand your concern about the additional cost. Many customers initially feel that way. Let me ask you this. If you could have just one of these coverages completely free, which would you choose? [Wait for response] That's interesting. That tells me you do see value in the protection itself. The good news is that for most customers, adding at least the service contract and GAP protection costs less per month than a few cups of coffee. At minimum, I strongly recommend these two essential coverages: the service contract because with so many moving parts in modern vehicles, it's not a matter of if something will need repair, but when; and GAP protection to safeguard your investment in case of a total loss. Doesn't it make sense to protect your investment?"

      Introducing GAP Coverage

      Script

      "There's one other important aspect of vehicle ownership I'd like to touch on briefly:

      • How familiar are you with how vehicle insurance works in the event of a total loss?
      • Many people don't realize that in the first few years of ownership, there can be a significant gap between what your insurance company would pay and what you might still owe on the vehicle.
      • Based on your down payment and term, this is something we should definitely address when we review your protection options."

      Transitioning to Protection Options

      Script

      "Great! It looks like our paperwork is just about ready. Before we move forward with the final steps, do you have any questions about what we've discussed or about your new [Vehicle Make/Model] in general?"

      Step 12Delivery & Follow-Up

      Complete the sale, vehicle orientation, and follow up after purchase.

      Draft content. Not from the source SOP. Review and edit with the sales lead before using in training.

      Pre-Delivery Inspection

      • Vehicle washed, vacuumed, full tank.
      • Both keys, owner's manual, registration in glove box.
      • Personal items removed from the trade.
      • Bow on the hood if you have one available.

      Vehicle Orientation

      Script

      "Before you drive off, I want to make sure you know how everything works. We'll spend about 15 minutes going through it together. Anything we don't cover, you can call or text me and I'll walk you through it."

      Cover at minimum:

      • Bluetooth pairing on their phone (do it with them).
      • Apple CarPlay or Android Auto if equipped.
      • Driver assist features: lane keep, cruise, blind-spot, park assist.
      • Climate, seat memory, mirror adjustments.
      • Fuel type, tire pressure, oil-change interval.
      • Key fob: remote start, panic, trunk.

      Hand Off to Service

      Script

      "Let me walk you over to our service team. They're the ones who'll take care of you for everything down the road, and I want them to put a face to the name now, not the first time you need an oil change."

      Ask for the Referral

      Script

      "I told you back when we started that my goal was to deliver your next three or four vehicles. I also want to earn the chance to take care of the people you care about. If a friend, family member, or coworker mentions they're shopping, would you give them my card and tell them to ask for me?"

      Follow-Up Cadence

      • Same day, evening. Text: "Made it home okay? Any questions on the way?"
      • Day 3. Phone call. How's the vehicle? Anything weird? Confirm first service is scheduled.
      • Day 7. Reminder about the 7 days to love it window. Reaffirm the choice.
      • Day 30. Check-in call. Ask for a Google review. Ask for a referral.
      • Day 90 and onward. Quarterly touch. Birthday, holidays, service reminders.
      Note: Delivery is when emotion peaks. The referral ask, the review ask, and the service hand-off all hit harder right now than they will in three days. Don't skip the orientation to save 15 minutes. A confused customer is the one who calls dissatisfied next week.
